Caps and floors

A cap is a strip of calls on an index's fixings, a floor a strip of puts. MakeCapFloor builds one from its type, a tenor and an Ibor index, as QuantLib's does: the strike, if not given, is the at-the-money rate of its floating leg.

Pricing on a volatility

Six five-year Euribor caps and floors on the curve of the curves guide, each priced on a 20% lognormal volatility and on a 0.60% normal one:

CapFloor capFloor = new MakeCapFloor(type, new Period(5, TimeUnit.Years), new Euribor6M(curve))
    .WithStrike(strike)
    .WithNominal(10e6)
    .ToCapFloor(context);

double black = BlackCapFloorEngine.Calculate(capFloor, context, curve, 0.20).NPV!.Value;
double bachelier = BachelierCapFloorEngine.Calculate(capFloor, context, curve, 0.0060).NPV!.Value;

5Y floor 3.00% 301,180.09 311,671.15

BlackCapFloorEngine prices each caplet by Black's formula, BachelierCapFloorEngine by Bachelier's. Each takes a volatility as a number, as here, or as an optionlet volatility structure: ConstantOptionletVolatility, or one stripped from a cap volatility surface (OptionletStripper1, StrippedOptionletAdapter). A shifted lognormal volatility takes its displacement as a further argument.
