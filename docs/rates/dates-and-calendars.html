Dates and calendars

Date is a day, Period a length of time in days, weeks, months or years, and a Calendar knows its market's business days. Each is an immutable value, as in QuantLib: new Date(23, Month.December, 2026), new Period(6, TimeUnit.Months).

Rolling dates

A calendar advances a date by business days or by a period, and adjusts a date that falls on a holiday by a business-day convention:

var calendar = new TARGET();
var trade = new Date(23, Month.December, 2026);
Date spot = calendar.Advance(trade, 2, TimeUnit.Days);
Date oneYear = calendar.Advance(spot, new Period(1, TimeUnit.Years), BusinessDayConvention.ModifiedFollowing);
Date endOfMonth = calendar.Adjust(new Date(31, Month.October, 2026), BusinessDayConvention.ModifiedFollowing);
Date Weekday Business day
2026-12-23 Wednesday yes
2026-12-28 Monday yes
2027-12-28 Tuesday yes
2026-10-30 Friday yes

Spot from 23 December is the 28th: TARGET closes on the 25th and 26th. The 31st of October 2026 is a Saturday, and modified following rolls it back to the Friday rather than into November.

The calendars are QuantLib's, each a sealed class: TARGET, UnitedStates and UnitedKingdom with their markets, and the rest by country. JointCalendar combines several, and BespokeCalendar is one of your own. Adding a holiday returns a new calendar, and leaves the original as it was. With the NanoQL.Extensions package a calendar's holidays can be changed as data (conventions.md).

Schedules and day counts

MakeSchedule builds a coupon schedule as QuantLib's does, and each day counter gives a period's accrual fraction:

Schedule schedule = new MakeSchedule()
    .From(spot)
    .To(calendar.Advance(spot, new Period(2, TimeUnit.Years)))
    .WithFrequency(Frequency.Semiannual)
    .WithCalendar(calendar)
    .WithConvention(BusinessDayConvention.ModifiedFollowing)
    .ToSchedule();

DayCounter[] dayCounters =
[
    new Actual360(), new Actual365Fixed(), new Thirty360(Thirty360Convention.BondBasis),
    new ActualActual(ActualActualConvention.ISDA),
];

Actual/Actual (ISDA) counts each day over its own year's length, so the periods in 2028, a leap year, differ from Actual/365 (Fixed).
