Remarks

The stripper strips the price surface at construction (Create(int, Calendar, BusinessDayConvention, DayCounter, Period, YoYCapFloorTermPriceSurface, Func<YoYInflationCapFloor, EvaluationContext, YoYOptionletVolatilitySurface, InstrumentResults>, YoYOptionletStripper, double, EvaluationContext, TInterpolator, VolatilityType, double)), in the context given, and the surface holds the result; a volatility is the strikes' interpolation of the stripped curves' volatilities at the time, extrapolated only when the surface allows it. The index is observed flat, at the start of its inflation period, as QuantLib's. QuantLib's memo of the last date's slice isn't ported: each query reads its slice.

A volatility reads the stripped curves at the surface's time for the observed date (Divergences/KInterpolatedYoYVolatilityTests.cs): each strike's stripped curve at that date when the surface is on the stripper's clock, the price surface's reference date and day counter, and on another clock the curves read early or late by the clocks' difference (on QuantLib's euro case, up to 2.7e-5 at 2 settlement days and 4.2e-5 on Actual/360, where QuantLib's lag error on the stripper's clock is 6.1e-4). QuantLib's turns the time back into a date, its whole years and the fraction's days at 365 a year, and reads each curve at that date through the curve's own lag, so the curves are read a lag early, and off Actual/365 a day or so further. QuantLib's case reads Dslice(Date), the curves' own date route, which is QuantLib's here; its protected date form, which only its time form calls, isn't ported.
