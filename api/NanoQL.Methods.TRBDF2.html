The TR-BDF2 scheme of the old finite-difference framework, over a TridiagonalOperator: a trapezoidal stage to t − αΔt, then a BDF2 stage to t − Δt, α = 2 − √2 (Bank et al.; http://ssrn.com/abstract=1648878). The operator must be linear.

Remarks

A working type, as the old framework's operators: it holds a copy of the operator, whose time it sets, and the boundary conditions it is given, and Step(Span<double>, double) writes into the caller's span. Its step's parts are built by SetStep(double); a step before it refuses, as QuantLib's operators of size 0 do.

For a time-dependent operator QuantLib builds the trapezoidal stage's implicit part at t − Δt, where the stage ends at t − αΔt, so it steps at first order (on u_t = (1 + t)u_xx, errors 0.21, 0.040, 0.0025, 0.0030 at 25 to 200 steps where TR-BDF2's are 0.25, 0.066, 0.017, 0.0041). Here the trapezoidal implicit part is built at t − αΔt and the BDF2 stage's at t − Δt (a documented divergence); for an operator constant in time, QuantLib's scheme, bit for bit.
