Remarks

QuantLib's class template over its binary function takes a delegate here. Its reference date and day counter are the first curve's, read when it is built, and so is its extrapolation flag, set when both curves extrapolate; its calendar, settlement days, maximum date and maximum time are the first curve's, read when they are asked for. Curves that change make a new curve: QuantLib's curve follows its handles.

QuantLib reads the second curve's zero rate at the composite's time, a time on the first curve's clock, which the second curve counts from its own reference date on its own day counter. Here the second curve is read at the instant that time denotes: its zero rate is the one implied over the composite's time by its discount from the composite's reference date to that instant, none accruing before its own reference date, the second curve restated on the first's clock. Where the two curves share their reference date and day counter, as QuantLib's case builds them, it is QuantLib's read and bits. A documented divergence.
