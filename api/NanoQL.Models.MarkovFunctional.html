Remarks

The model needs an input smile that is arbitrage free, smooth (at least a C¹ call price function) and whose call prices fall fast enough in the strike. Kahale's method, or a SABR smile made arbitrage free by it, may pretreat the smile; either implies zero density for rates below minus the shift, so the curve's at-the-money forwards must lie above it. Without a pretreatment the smile must be arbitrage free and cover the strikes from the lower to the upper rate bound. The calibration assumes one curve, the model's, throughout; a swap's floating leg is P(t, T₀) − P(t, T₁), and the calibration instruments start at their fixing dates. A shifted smile's lower rate bound moves by the shift. A custom smile inverts the digitals itself.

Immutable, a Gaussian1dModel implementing ICalibratedModel over its volatilities: it calibrates its numeraire when it is built, under the context it is given (the playbook's LazyObject row), where QuantLib's calibrates at its first use under the evaluation date then. WithParams(Vector) keeps the smiles and re-tabulates the numeraire, QuantLib's generateArguments; ForceArbitrageIndices(ReadOnlySpan<(int Left, int Right)>, EvaluationContext) returns a copy calibrated again; ModelOutputs(EvaluationContext) computes the diagnostics per call. Its first volatility is fixed by default in a calibration (DefaultFixParameters), as QuantLib's calibrate fixes it: the prices don't depend on the volatilities' level.

Six of QuantLib's defects are fixed. A caplet calibration point's end date, and the outputs' forward rates' and caplets', are the index's maturity date for the value date, as QuantLib's master computes the Gaussian1d forward rate after the pin (#2848), where QuantLib advances the value date by the tenor itself (markovfunctional.cpp:278, :949, :1109). With flat payoff extrapolation, the right tails integrate the last node's value, where QuantLib's integrate the second-last's (:516-519, :1064-1066, :1141-1143). A curve given to the numeraire is read at the numeraire time with extrapolation, as the model's own is (:775-785), and a curve on another clock at the instant QuantLib's argument denotes, by the date a time comes from; and the model's own curve, which the ratio with a given curve reads, with extrapolation, as the calibration reads it (:783-785, :797-799), so a model whose calibration extrapolates its curve prices with a given curve too. The settings refuse any two smile pretreatments (MarkovFunctionalModelSettings), and the state process is its equation's (MfStateProcess), its variance continuous in a small reversion.

From QuantLib's master after the pin (#2912, Divergences/MarkovFunctionalUpstreamFixesTests.cs): the upper rate bound's digital is struck at the bound, as the rest of the model reads it, where QuantLib 1.44 subtracts the smile's shift (:432-436), so a shifted smile capped its rates below the bound; and the settings ask for two grid points, the master's minimum.

Remarks

QuantLib's setParams with its generateArguments: the state process takes the new volatilities and the numeraire is tabulated again, the smiles and the calibration points kept. As QuantLib's, the parameters aren't checked against the arguments' constraints, and the refusals of a wrong size come before any write.
