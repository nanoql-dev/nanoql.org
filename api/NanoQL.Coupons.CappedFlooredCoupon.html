Remarks

The payoff of a capped coupon is N T min(a L + b, C), of a floored one N T max(a L + b, F), and of a collared one N T min(max(a L + b, F), C), where N is the nominal, T the accrual time, L the floating rate, a its gearing, b the spread, and C and F the strikes. A capped rate decomposes as (a L + b) + |a| min((C − b) / |a| − ξ L, 0), with ξ = sgn(a): the swaplet's rate less a caplet on the fixing, struck at the effective cap (C − b) / a, and at a negative gearing the cap becomes a floor on the fixing, and the floor a cap.

The rate is the underlying's swaplet rate plus the floorlet's minus the caplet's, all through the underlying's own pricer, read from one view of its rates for the call, so a pricer whose per-coupon state costs more than its queries builds it once, as QuantLib's one initialize serves the three. A copy with another pricer (SetPricer<T>(T, FloatingRateCouponPricer?)) carries the pricer into a copy of the underlying too, as QuantLib's setPricer sets both. The coupon copies the underlying's terms when it is built, its nominal from ContractNominal, since a constructor has no context.

Unsealed, as in QuantLib, where the capped Ibor and CMS coupons derive from it.
