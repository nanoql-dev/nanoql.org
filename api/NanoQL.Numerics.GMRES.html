Remarks

QuantLib stops at a breakdown, the new Krylov vector's norm below ε², without rotating the column that found it, and solves with the columns before it (gmres.cpp:101-131). At the first step there are none: the back-substitution writes before its array, undefined (the process crashes on diag(2, 3) with b = (1, 0), an eigenvector). At a later step the column dropped turns a solved system into a refusal: with A = diag(1, 1, 3, 3) and b = (1, 1, 1, 1), the vector is exactly zero at the second step, and solve throws "could not converge" where solveWithRestart(5) returns (1, 1, 1/3, 1/3). Here, as the method defines the breakdown, the Krylov space holds the solution: the column is rotated and the system solved with it. A documented divergence.

A restart count of zero is refused: QuantLib's bound restart - 1 wraps to unlimited restarts (gmres.cpp:54), where every positive count bounds them, and it refuses a zero bound on the iterations.
