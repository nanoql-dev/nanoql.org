Remarks

The day count is the number of business days between two dates on the calendar, by QuantLib's walk over months and years. QuantLib keeps each month's count in a cache keyed by the calendar's name and shared by the whole process, filled once and never invalidated, so a count goes stale after a holiday edit and two calendars of one name share their counts (business252.cpp:31-68). Here the constructor counts each month of 1901 to 2199 once, on its own calendar, into a table no other object shares; the counts are QuantLib's wherever its cache isn't stale, a documented divergence where it is.

A month the calendar refuses (a calendar with no data for a year) is left out of the table, with the rest of its year, and counted when a count reads it, as QuantLib's walk counts it, so the calendar's refusal comes where QuantLib's does.

Two Business/252 day counters are equal when their calendars are (by type, name, and added and removed holidays), where QuantLib compares their names alone.
