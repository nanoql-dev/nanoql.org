Remarks

Immutable. QuantLib's helper computes its market value lazily, at its first use; here a derived helper's constructor computes it, under the context it is given, through ComputeMarketValue(EvaluationContext).

A helper of one's own. The base's constructor computes nothing: a derived constructor first builds what its BlackPrice(double, EvaluationContext) reads, then calls ComputeMarketValue(EvaluationContext) last, as QuantLib's derived helpers call the base's performCalculations last in their own. C# runs a further override of BlackPrice(double, EvaluationContext), called from that constructor, before the further class's constructor has run, so the class whose constructor calls ComputeMarketValue(EvaluationContext) seals BlackPrice(double, EvaluationContext) (or is sealed, as SwaptionHelper is).

An extension point, as in QuantLib: the swaption helper derives from it, as later ports' cap and Heston helpers will.

Remarks

For the implied-volatility error, the model value is clamped to the volatilities 0.001 and 10 for a shifted lognormal volatility, 0.00005 and 0.5 for a normal one: the volatility is the bound when the model value is at or beyond the Black price there, and is solved with an accuracy of 1e-12 in 5,000 evaluations otherwise.

Exceptions

ArgumentNullException

model or context is null.

NanoQLException

The instrument can't be priced, or the implied volatility not solved.
