Remarks

QuantLib's template on the tree: Calculate<TTree>(ConvertibleBond, EvaluationContext, GeneralizedBlackScholesProcess, int, double, ImmutableArray<Dividend>) takes the tree as its type argument; the seven named engines, as QuantLib-Python names them, fix it. The credit spread is a number, where QuantLib observes a quote.

Its clock: the tree runs from the bond's settlement date over the risk-free curve's year fraction to the last exercise date, and the NPV is the bond's value there, the settlement value equal to it, as QuantLib's are: a named departure from the playbook's lattice clock, since the engine prices the bond at its settlement date and QuantLib's convertible cases assert that definition. The structures are flattened at the last exercise date, dated at the risk-free curve's reference date, as QuantLib's are; where the dividend curve or the volatility has another day counter or reference date than the risk-free curve, its rate or volatility is the one that gives its discount or variance from the risk-free curve's reference date to that date, counted on the risk-free day counter, each structure accruing nothing before its own date, where QuantLib reads it on its own day counter and the tree reads it as the risk-free curve's (Divergences/ConvertibleClockTests.cs). Where every structure shares the risk-free curve's day counter and reference date, the tree is QuantLib's.

The tree's stock starts at the settlement date's forward, the spot less the dividends' present values divided by the risk-free discount to the settlement date, as QuantLib's master starts it after 1.44 (#2752, issue #2701), where QuantLib 1.44 starts it at the evaluation date's spot, so a bond with a settlement lag converts on a spot short of the carry over the lag (Divergences/ConvertibleUpstreamFixesTests.cs).

The time grid and the lattice take their step count from the tree, its columns less one, where QuantLib's take the count given: the Leisen-Reimer and Joshi trees build an even count on the next odd one, as the binomial vanilla engine handles them. Every odd count and every other tree is QuantLib's.
