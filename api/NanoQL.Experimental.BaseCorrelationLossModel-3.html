Remarks

The literature is immense; beyond the introductory level (precrisis) see chapters 19, 20 and 21 of Dominic O'Kane, "Modelling single name and multi-name credit derivatives", Wiley Finance, 2008. Freely available: "Credit Correlation: A Guide", JP Morgan Credit Derivatives Strategy, 12 March 2004; "Introducing Base Correlations", JP Morgan, 22 March 2004; "A Relative Value Framework for Credit Correlation", JP Morgan, 27 April 2004; "Valuing and Hedging Synthetic CDO Tranches Using Base Correlations", Bear Stearns, 17 May 2004; "Correlation Primer", Nomura Fixed Income Research, 6 August 2004; "Base Correlation Explained", Lehman Brothers Fixed Income Quantitative Credit Research, 15 November 2004; "Pricing CDOs with a smile", Societe Generale Credit Research, February 2005; and for bespoke base correlation "Base Correlation Mapping", Lehman Brothers' Quantitative Credit Research Quarterly, Volume 2007-Q1. Typical postcrisis data is in JP Morgan's Global Correlation Daily Analytics.

The crisis problems, pricing stressed portfolios or tranches over the maximum loss, are the base models' responsibility: users select a base model accordingly, a copula or a random loss given default one. This is not a bespoke base correlation model (bespoke as to the basket's composition, not just its attachment levels), whose loss interpolation is on the expected loss to match the two baskets: the surface should refer to the basket priced. To be done, as in QuantLib: bespoke portfolio models. Most statistics are not implemented, not impossible, but the model is intended for pricing rather than portfolio risk management.

The base models are QuantLib's four setupModels specialisations, each a type implementing IBaseCorrelationModel<TInitTraits>, and immutable: each statistic builds the two at its correlations, where QuantLib's sets the correlation quotes they observe. Bound to a basket with realized defaults, the equity baskets hold the live names, their notionals and recoveries, over the restated tranche: QuantLib's take the live notionals on the whole pool, which its basket refuses ("unmatched data entry sizes in basket"; Divergences/BaseCorrelationLossModelTests.cs). A correlation the surface gives outside [0, 1) is refused, naming the date and level, where QuantLib's base model returns NaN below 0 and refuses above 1. An equity tranche's attachment leg is 0, its basket [0, 0] not priced: QuantLib's prices it, so its LHP base refuses where the surface extrapolates below 0 at level 0 and its inhomogeneous pool base throws on every equity tranche ("a out of range", its own \todo: "is failing for equity tranches at least").
