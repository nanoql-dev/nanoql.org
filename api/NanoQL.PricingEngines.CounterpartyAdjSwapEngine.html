The bilateral counterparty-risk adjusted swap engine: the swap's NPV less the counterparty's expected loss on its exposure, plus the investor's, each exposure a sequence of swaptions at the swap's fixed rate, one per fixed period, weighted by the party's default probability over the period.

Remarks

Collateral is not considered, nor wrong-way risk: rates and default are uncorrelated (Sorensen and Bollier, "Pricing swap default risk", Financial Analysts Journal, 1994; Brigo and Masetti, 2004 and 2005). The fair rate is QuantLib's approximation, not an iteration.

QuantLib's three constructors are the overloads of Calculate(FixedVsFloatingSwap, EvaluationContext, YieldTermStructure, Func<Swaption, EvaluationContext, InstrumentResults>, DefaultProbabilityTermStructure, double, DefaultProbabilityTermStructure?, double), its volatility quote and constant volatility one overload; with no investor curve, QuantLib's default-free one, a flat hazard rate of 1e-12 on the counterparty curve's day counter, is built per call under the context. QuantLib's loop over the fixed payment dates skips those before the counterparty curve's reference date with no end check (cvaswapengine.cpp:128), reading past the dates when every one is before it; here it stops at the end, and the swap is then worth its NPV.

QuantLib strikes every swaptionlet and its reverse at the swap's risk-free fair rate (cvaswapengine.cpp:147, :170, :179), so its adjustment is the same whatever the swap's fixed rate: a swap away from par takes the at-the-money swap's CVA and DVA. Here the NPV's are struck at the swap's fixed rate, the exposure of the contract itself, which is QuantLib's within a few ulps where the fixed rate is the fair rate. The fair rate keeps QuantLib's: its first-order root of the adjusted NPV, taken on swaptionlets struck at the risk-free fair rate, the market's, so the same whatever the fixed rate; on the contract's strikes it would move with the fixed rate, away from the root off par.
