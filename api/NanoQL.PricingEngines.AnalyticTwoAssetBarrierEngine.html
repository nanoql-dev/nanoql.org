Remarks

The value alone, as QuantLib's. The bivariate normal is QuantLib's default, BivariateCumulativeNormalDistributionWe04DP, where QuantLib's engine reads Drezner's 1978 algorithm, to six decimal places; on QuantLib's cases the values move by at most 6.7e-6, within QuantLib's tolerance.

Five documented divergences. Each asset's carry reads its own process's curves and the payoff is discounted on the first process's risk-free curve, where QuantLib takes the second asset's carry from the first process's rate (Divergences/TwoAssetBarrierTwoCurvesTests.cs). The knock-in's vanilla carries the first asset's dividends, S₁e^((b₁−r)T)N(d₁), where QuantLib's drops them, so in-out parity holds (Divergences/TwoAssetBarrierDividendTests.cs). The second asset's volatility is read at the barrier, its own price level, where QuantLib reads it at the first asset's strike (Divergences/TwoAssetBarrierSmileTests.cs). And every rate and volatility is read over the time to expiry on the clock across both processes (NanoQL.Methods.FdmBlackScholesOp.ClockAcross(System.Collections.Generic.IReadOnlyList{NanoQL.Processes.GeneralizedBlackScholesProcess})), each structure at the instants that time denotes, accruing nothing before its date, where QuantLib reads everything at the first process's time; a structure sharing the clock's times is read as QuantLib reads it (Divergences/BarrierClosedFormMismatchedTimesTests.cs). And a non-European exercise is refused, as the family's European engines refuse it, where QuantLib prices it to its last date (Divergences/TwoAssetBarrierExerciseTests.cs).

The formula is a first-passage closed form in equivalent-constant rates and volatilities, the variance and discounts to expiry: no exact closed form exists for a first passage under general deterministic term structures, and its single correlation term, ρσ₁σ₂, can't carry a first volatility that moves over the option's life. On shaped markets (1.5 years; forward rates 1%, 3%, 6%, dividends 0%, 1%, 3% and volatilities 15%, 25%, 35%, rising or falling; ρ 0.5) it misses an independent Monte Carlo by −24% to +21%, and by 3% to 7% with the first volatility flat, where on a flat market it agrees. A Monte Carlo on the two processes is the term-structure-exact route.
