The Black (1976) calculator: the value and sensitivities of a striked payoff on a forward with a log-normal distribution of a given standard deviation, discounted. It supports plain vanilla, cash-or-nothing, asset-or-nothing and gap payoffs.

Remarks

Four of QuantLib's results are fixed, documented divergences, each checked by QuantLib's own second route to the value:

  • At zero volatility, QuantLib's delta, forward delta and strike sensitivity tell a call from a put by alpha >= 0 (blackcalculator.cpp:214-228, :256-270, :438-452), but alpha is 0 for an out-of-the-money put and for every cash-or-nothing payoff, which then get a call's delta. Here they are the nonzero branch's formulas with the derivative terms zero, from alpha and beta, so the theta, the theta per day, the elasticity, the rho and the dividend rho that read them are fixed too.
  • QuantLib's volga divides by the standard deviation σ√T where the derivative in σ divides by σ (:511-523), so it is the volga over √T: here it is multiplied by √T.
  • QuantLib's vanna and volga use the plain vanilla's closed form for every payoff (:494-523); for other payoffs they are here the derivatives of QuantLib's vega through the payoff's alpha and beta. The plain vanilla's are QuantLib's formulas.
  • QuantLib's in-the-money probabilities are N(d2) and N(d1) for every payoff (blackcalculator.hpp:129-135), a call's; a put's are here 1 − N(d2) and 1 − N(d1), the expressions QuantLib's cash-or-nothing and asset-or-nothing puts value.

Unsealed: QuantLib's Black-Scholes calculator derives from it (blackscholescalculator.hpp:32), reading only its public members.
