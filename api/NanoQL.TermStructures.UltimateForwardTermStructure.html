Remarks

Past the cut-off point T_c, the first smoothing point, the forward rate from T_c to T is UFR + (LLFR − UFR)·B(T − T_c), with B(x) = (1 − e^(−αx))/(αx) and α the growth factor. The documentation is the Dutch Central Bank's: the FTK term structure documentation (Financieel toetsingskader, https://www.dnb.nl/media/4lmprzrk/vaststelling_methode_rentetermijnstructuur_ftk.pdf), the UFR 2013-2019 term structure documentation (https://www.dnb.nl/media/0vmbxaf4/methodologie-dnb.pdf) and the UFR 2023 term structure documentation (p. 46, https://www.tweedekamer.nl/downloads/document?id=2022D50944). The zero rates may be rounded to a number of decimal places in a given compounding: rounding a rate of 1.5555% to 5 places gives 0.01556, 1.556%.

QuantLib's curve derives from RelativeDerivedYieldTermStructure<ZeroYieldStructure>, a class template over its own base, which C# can't express; it implements the template's members itself, as ZeroSpreadedTermStructure does. Its reference date and day counter are the original's, read when it is built, and so is its extrapolation flag; its calendar and settlement days are the original's, read when they are asked for. Its maximum date is the latest date. Quotes or an original that change make a new curve: QuantLib's curve follows its quotes and its handle. The rounding is built with the curve, so a precision it refuses refuses the curve.

Two documented divergences. QuantLib's B(x) is unguarded: α = 0 gives 0/0 and the curve throws "positive compound factor required", and α = 1e-12 puts the 60-year zero rate 9e-9 below its limit; here it is −expm1(−αx)/(αx), and 1 at αx = 0, its limit, as upstream's own oneMinusExpOverX after the pin. QuantLib reads the original without extrapolation (ultimateforwardtermstructure.hpp:168, :173), so past a base that doesn't extrapolate the curve throws although its maximum date is the latest date, where the zero- and forward-spreaded curves extrapolate; here it reads the original with extrapolation on, as theirs do.
