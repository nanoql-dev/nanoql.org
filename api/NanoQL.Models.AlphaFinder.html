Remarks

A working type: each solve keeps its inputs, as QuantLib's members, and sets the form's α; the alpha calibration builds one per calibration around its own copy of the form. It isn't thread-safe.

QuantLib's master's finder (#2910; Divergences/AlphaFinderTests.cs), which fixes four of QuantLib 1.44's defects: the downward bracket search stops at its first acceptable α, testing the value it moves, where 1.44's tests the upward one's (alphafinder.cpp:372-373); the minimization's feasibility test reads the trial α, where 1.44's reads the objective's value there (:117, :145); the bisection returns at an exact root and where its point no longer moves; and Solve(double, int, ReadOnlySpan<double>, ReadOnlySpan<double>, ReadOnlySpan<double>, double, double, double, double, double, double, int, out double, out double, out double, Span<double>) and SolveWithMaxHomogeneity(double, int, ReadOnlySpan<double>, ReadOnlySpan<double>, ReadOnlySpan<double>, double, double, double, double, double, double, int, out double, out double, out double, Span<double>) return their final part's success, where 1.44's return true where it fails, at α₀ or at the search's α (:335-346, :405-415, :550-561), the swap rate's last volatility then left as the caller's. Two of nanoql's own: the searches and the minimization also stop where their point no longer moves, where QuantLib's never end at a tolerance of 0 (:30-111); and b is NaN where the final part fails, where QuantLib leaves the caller's value. The alpha calibration's fallback minimizes on 1.44's test of the objective's value. Inputs shorter than the step index reads, which QuantLib reads past, are refused.
