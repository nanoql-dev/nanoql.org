Remarks

A mutable working type (§2.7), QuantLib's API: values or densities are added, then Normalize(), which the reads call, computes the cumulative and excess probabilities. The density is uniform within a bucket, as CumulativeDensity(double)'s interpolation reads it. QuantLib's default constructor leaves the size uninitialized; a distribution here is built of its buckets.

Three members depart from QuantLib's, each fixed on that density (Divergences/DistributionMembersTests.cs): CumulativeDensity(double) in the first bucket interpolates from 0, where QuantLib's reads the cumulative at index −1 (distribution.cpp:220-230); ExpectedShortfall(double) integrates the density above the quantile, where QuantLib's sums the left edges from the bucket after the quantile's over a denominator leaving its first term out (5.625 and 9 for a uniform's 5.25 and 9.5, :328-341); and Tranche(double, double) keeps the mass above the detachment in the last bucket, where QuantLib's sets the last cumulative to 1 before computing the last density from it, 0 (a total probability of 0.7, :236-284).

A tranched distribution's buckets end at the tranche's width D − A: the bucket spanning it is uniform up to it, and the buckets Tranche(double, double) truncates to it, the mass above the detachment among them, are an atom there, as CumulativeDensity(double) and ExpectedShortfall(double) read them. QuantLib's keeps their widths, which its left-edge shortfall never reads; the uniform shortfall read past the tranche (3.5 for 3 at 0.5 on a uniform's [2, 5] tranche).
