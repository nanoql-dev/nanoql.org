The saddle-point portfolio credit default loss model: the saddle-point expansion's integrations of several default risk metrics, the dependence dealt with through a latent model that makes the integrals conditional on its factor, the latent variables integrated indirectly.

Remarks

See R. Martin, K. Thompson and C. Browne, "Taking to the saddle", Risk, June 2001, p. 91; R. Martin, "The saddlepoint method and portfolio optionalities", Risk, December 2006; R. Martin, K. Thompson and C. Browne, "VAR: who contributes and how much?", Risk, August 2001; R. J. Martin, "Shortfall: Who contributes and how much?", Credit Suisse, 3 January 2007; J. Annaert, C. Garcia Joao Batista, J. Lamoot and G. Lanine, "Don't Fall from the Saddle: the Importance of Higher Moments of Credit Loss Distributions", Gent University, February 2006; A. Antonov, S. Mechkov and T. Misirpashaev, "Analytical techniques for synthetic CDOs and credit default risk measures", NumeriX, 23 May 2005; X. Huang, C. W. Oosterlee and M. Mesters, "Computation of VaR and VaR contribution in the Vasicek portfolio credit loss model: a comparative study", Journal of Credit Risk 3(3), Fall 2007, and "Higher-order saddlepoint approximations in the Vasicek portfolio credit loss model", Journal of Computational Finance 11(1), Fall 2007. A high order expansion is used, more expensive; see Antonov et al. for the terms retained. A. Owen, A. McLeod and K. Thompson's hybrid saddlepoint method fixes the error at low loss levels (Risk, August 2009), not implemented here; for the general theory, R. W. Butler, "Saddlepoint approximations with applications", Cambridge, 2007. To be done, as in QuantLib: some portfolios' instabilities in the high order terms; calls avoiding the copula inversions; the saddle point search's convergence in extreme cases (very high factors, factors not ordered from high to low). Random recoveries would have to be passed to the conditional methods as the probabilities are.

Kept, QuantLib's method: the expected tranche loss is the tranche of the conditional expected loss, a large-pool reading, not a saddle-point expansion; at correlation 0 on 100 names its four tranches read 292.78, 0, 0, 0 where the exact recursive model reads 245.40, 46.48, 0.89, 0.0003.

The tail's degenerate nodes and domain (Divergences/SaddlePointLossModelTests.cs). QuantLib's saddle search brackets on the name of the largest loss, taking the logarithm of a ratio of its conditional probability p and 1 − p, so a node where that name is at 0 or 1 throws ("invalid range"): at the evaluation date every probability is 0, and on ten names every correlation from 0.9 throws. Here the bracket's name is the largest among those strictly between 0 and 1, the certain ones carried by the cumulants (a name at 1 a linear term, one at 0 nothing), and a node where every name is at 0 or 1 reads its exact conditional loss: its tail 1 or 0, its density 0, its split and shortfall exact. Where QuantLib's throws (100 names, hazard 0.03, recovery 0.4), the tail at 10% of the pool reads the exact (recursive) model's within 1.2% through correlation 0.9 and within 5% at 0.95, below it (5.0% at three months), the expansion's own accuracy on near-comonotonic pools, where QuantLib's runs reading it within 1% at 0.7 and 0.75. Refused, naming the saddle-point expansion: a tail outside [0, 1] (beyond the quadrature's rounding, 1e-12), and a largest relative loss at or below the bracket's 1e-5, whose logarithm QuantLib takes of a negative number. The factor nodes' tails are QuantLib's, inside [0, 1] or not: at low losses single nodes read up to 6.31 or slightly below 0 where the integrated tail and the percentile are right (on 100 names, hazard 0.03, the percentile at 50%, 90% and 99% within the exact distribution's loss unit at correlations 0.1 to 0.5 and 30 days to ten years). Kept, the method's domain: on small pools the expansion misreads the tail inside [0, 1], on ten names at correlation 0.3 P(L ≥ 1%) 0.983 for an exact 0.0223 at a month and 0.754 for 0.603 at five years, where 100 names read 0.4389 for 0.4387 at a year; outside it (26.78 a day out at correlation 0.548, 2.61 at a week on ten names) the refusal.

The saddle search. QuantLib's brackets the point on the name of the largest loss and, below or above the bracket's losses, returns the bracket's end instead of solving, so on pools whose largest loss is a name unlikely to default it reads up to 40 times the exact tail (0.978 for 0.198 at 1% of 100 names a year out at correlation 0.7, 0.2008 for 0.0049 at 10% at 0.9); here the bracket widens until it holds the level and the point is solved, QuantLib's search wherever its bracket holds it. The cumulants keep QuantLib's arithmetic but for names at 0, out of the sums, names at 1, their linear terms, and where QuantLib's form overflows the logistic form, so a widened point reads no 0·∞; the split's contributions take the same treatment, where QuantLib's form reads ∞/∞. Above the largest loss a factor node reaches, its names' losses given default but those certain not to default, no point exists: the tail reads 0 there by QuantLib's own return above 1 less the average recovery, and the density and the conditional shortfall read 0 too, where at the widened end QuantLib's expressions read 0/0, so the shortfall of a tranche detaching above the pool's largest loss was NaN (QuantLib's TO DO list: "Failing when the tranche upper loss limit goes over the max attainable loss"). Divergences/SaddlePointLossModelTests.cs.

ProbOverLoss(Date, double) reads 0 above the whole tranche, where QuantLib's compares the fraction with the detachment amount; ExpectedShortfall(Date, double) is the tranche loss's, at most the tranche's remaining notional, where QuantLib's integrates the whole portfolio's tail above the tranche's percentile and caps it at the tranche notional times the remaining ratio (9 for a 300 tranche; "ESF <- tranched????!!" in its TO DO list). Bound to a basket with realized defaults, the model prices its live names over the restated tranche.
