The engine for European average-basket options whose assets are all driven by one stochastic factor: each asset's deviation is its Black volatility at the spot, signed, times the square root of the time.

Remarks

J. Choi, "Sum of all Black-Scholes-Merton Models: An efficient Pricing Method for Spread, Basket and Asian Options", https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.03172. The price is closed form at the root of a sum of exponentials, found by Brent's solver, and the root's d is returned among the additional results as "d", as QuantLib's.

The processes must discount alike: QuantLib's check that every risk-free discount is close enough to the first's ("interest rates need to be the same for all underlyings"). The structures are read by date, accruing nothing before their dates, where QuantLib's reads throw for an option expiring before a later structure's date (Divergences/BasketExpiryBeforeReferenceDateTests.cs).
