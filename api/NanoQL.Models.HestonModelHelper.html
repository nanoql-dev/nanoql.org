Remarks

Immutable. The constructor builds what QuantLib's helper builds at its first calculation: the exercise date, the period advanced on the calendar from the risk-free curve's reference date; the maturity, the time to it (below); the option, a call where the discounted strike is at least the discounted spot and a put otherwise; and the market value, the Black price at the quoted volatility over the maturity. QuantLib's spot is a quote or a number; here it is the number.

The model value is the pricing engine's: AnalyticHestonEngine with its defaults, until SetPricingEngine<TModel>(Func<VanillaOption, EvaluationContext, TModel, InstrumentResults>?) sets another, which takes the model being calibrated as its last argument; with no engine, pricing an option that hasn't expired refuses with QuantLib's "null pricing engine".

The Black price and the option's type read both curves by the exercise date, as the engine's forward does. QuantLib reads them at the risk-free curve's time to it, which on a dividend curve with another day counter or reference date is another date, so its market value and its model value price different forwards (Divergences/HestonForwardByDateTests.cs). Where the curves share the day counter and the reference date, the two reads are the same.

The maturity runs from the earlier curve's reference date, as the engine's model time does, so beside a risk-free curve settling later the Black price's variance accrues from the evaluation date, as QuantLib's helper on the market restated from that date reads it; QuantLib's runs from the risk-free curve's date (Divergences/HestonMismatchedTimesTests.cs).
