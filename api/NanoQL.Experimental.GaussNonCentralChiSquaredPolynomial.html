Remarks

QuantLib sums each ordinary moment's closed form in double and runs MomentBasedGaussianPolynomial's recursion in double; the map from ordinary moments to the recurrence is ill-conditioned (Gautschi), so at QuantLib's own CLV cases its 14 nodes are 2.6e-2 and 0.61 relative off the exact ones, at λ 50 and 75 Boost refuses a negative node and at λ 100 it doesn't return. Here the moments are exact, from the cumulants 2^(j−1)(j−1)!(ν + jλ) on ν and λ as the binary fractions they are, QuantLib's recursion runs on those exact rationals, and α and β are rounded to double once: the nodes are within a few ulps of the exact ones at every λ. QuantLib's recursion is a template on its number type for this, tested on a multi-precision type; exact rationals are that precision's limit (Divergences/NonCentralChiSquaredPolynomialTests.cs). Moment(int) is the exact moment rounded once.

As QuantLib's, the moments stop at the 27th, so a rule takes at most 14 nodes. The recursion's values are memoised as the base memoises its own, under a lock; a 14-node rule's numbers reach about 9,000 bits.
