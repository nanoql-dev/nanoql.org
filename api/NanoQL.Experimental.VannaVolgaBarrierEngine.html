The Vanna-Volga engine for FX barrier options (Castagna and Mercurio, 2007): the analytic barrier price at the money, corrected by the vanna and volga of the 25-delta call and put, weighted by the probability of not touching the barrier, and bounded by the vanilla price the Vanna-Volga smile gives.

Remarks

QuantLib's method, its quote shifts for the barrier's vega, volga and vanna replayed on local values in QuantLib's order (each shift and set-back made on the value the quote holds, since a + s − s need not be a), its barrier prices the analytic engine's as nanoql fixes it. Its additional results carry QuantLib's tags: "Forward", "StrikeVol", "VanillaPrice", "BarrierInPrice", "BarrierOutPrice" and, inside the barriers, "lambda".

Two changes, each a documented divergence that leaves QuantLib's bits where it doesn't apply. The engine reads each curve at the instant the quotes' time denotes on the volatility's clock (dated by the evaluation date on Actual/365F), nothing accrued before the curve's own date, where QuantLib reads each at that time on its own clock: its discount factors and the touch probability's zero rates (the logarithms of those discounts over the time). And a rebate is priced as each option pays it: an out option's price with its rebate is QuantLib's, but where QuantLib's bounds bind its rebate-free price, the bound plus the rebate's value at the hit; an in option is QuantLib's rebate-free in price plus the analytic engine's value of the rebate paid at expiry; at or beyond the barrier an out option is its rebate, due now. QuantLib prices a knock-in by parity with the knock-out's rebate and bounds the out price as though it had none. At a zero rebate every added term is 0.
