Remarks

A call has QuantLib's one-dimensional integral. The engine reads the risk-free curve on the model's clock, the earliest reference date of the process's curves on the risk-free day counter, the maturity's time from it and the rate the curve accrues to the maturity per unit of that time, where QuantLib measures the time from the evaluation date; on curves dated on the evaluation date QuantLib's numbers stand.

Documented divergences (Divergences/IntegralHestonVarianceOptionTests.cs). A put is the call's integral without its forward term, the integrated variance's mean less the strike, by parity, and other payoffs are refused, where QuantLib sums any payoff but a call against the integrated variance's density on 4,096 points of spacing √(2π)/64, about one standard deviation of it: QuantLib's put case prices 0.0466796 against 0.0465915, a forward-start put 2.6% low, a cash-or-nothing put 13% to 24% low and above 1 at a strike past the distribution. The process's dividend curve isn't read, as the integrated variance's law doesn't depend on the drift, where QuantLib refuses a process whose dividend handle isn't empty. An option starting after the clock's origin is priced on the variance integrated from its start: each integrand is affine in the variance at the start, so its expectation over that variance's non-central χ² law from v₀ is the CIR transform, E[e^(Bv_s)] = (1 − 2cB)^(−d/2) exp(Bv₀e^(−κs)/(1 − 2cB)), c = σ²(1 − e^(−κs))/(4κ), d = 4κθ/σ², and the mean term takes E[v_s] = θ + (v₀ − θ)e^(−κs), where QuantLib prices every option as starting today; a seasoned option, its start before the evaluation date, is refused, as QuantLib's header says.
