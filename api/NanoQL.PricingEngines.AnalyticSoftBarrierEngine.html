Remarks

The value alone, as QuantLib's. A soft barrier option is the average of the single-barrier options with barriers in [L, U], as upstream's own test states it (#2883's testSoftBarrierDirection); its knock-in is computed here as that average of AnalyticBarrierEngine's standard knock-ins, (1/(U − L))∫ DI(H) dH, the barriers on the spot's far side counted as knocked in, by the adaptive Gauss-Kronrod rule split at the strike, the spot and the forward and at one to thirty transition widths either side of the last two, at the tolerance's measure on the average, and its knock-out as the vanilla less it.

Haug's form integrates the standard knock-in's branch for a barrier at or below the strike, so it is the average only for a down barrier on a call with U ≤ K, or an up barrier on a put with L ≥ K, the spot outside the band; it is taken there, with QuantLib's bits, where it is finite, its carry b = r − q is 2e-3 or more from 0 and from −σ², where its terms cancel, and the band's relative width (U − L)/U is 3e-3 or more, below which its difference quotient cancels. Elsewhere the average is taken (documented divergences; Divergences/SoftBarrier*Tests.cs): QuantLib's nudge of the rate at r ≈ q, its rebuild of flat Act/360 curves for a band narrower than 1e-4 in price, the NaN of its overflowing powers at small volatility, and its values outside the form's domain are superseded. Upstream's refusal of the directions the form doesn't cover (#2883) isn't taken: they are priced by the average (Divergences/BarrierUpstreamFixesTests.cs). A band of zero width is the analytic barrier engine's single barrier. QuantLib's limits on the rates, which no formula needs, aren't ported. Each rate is read on the volatility's clock and each time is the volatility's, as the analytic barrier engine reads them.

The exercise must be European, as the option's documentation states and the average's engine requires; QuantLib's engine doesn't check it and prices any exercise to its last date (Divergences/SoftBarrierExerciseTests.cs).

Haug's form and the average are first-passage closed forms in equivalent-constant rates and a volatility, the variance and discounts to expiry: no exact closed form exists for a first passage under general deterministic term structures, and on shaped markets they miss the average of FdBlackScholesBarrierEngine's single-barrier knock-ins by -0.8% to +1.5%; that average is the term-structure-exact route.
