The Heston stochastic-local volatility model calibrated by Monte Carlo: at each time step the calibration paths' spots are sorted into bins, and the leverage at each bin's midpoint is the local volatility over the root of the bin's mean variance.

Remarks

Immutable: the calibration runs at construction, where QuantLib's model calibrates at its first read; its draws are kept per path and step in one array as QuantLib keeps them. The leverage function is FixedLocalVolSurface on the calibrated values.

The calibration's paths read the leverage at each step's start, a node of the surface being calibrated, as QuantLib 1.44 reads it there, beyond the time's strikes by the time's line (NanoQL.TermStructures.FixedLocalVolWorkingSurface), so the calibration is QuantLib's bit for bit; the surface the model returns holds a strike beyond a time's strikes at the nearest one, at a node as between nodes. The local volatility is read at each step's end, where QuantLib 1.44 reads it at the step's start (QuantLib's #2860).

The model's times are a clock's, from the earliest reference date of the curves and the local volatility on the risk-free curve's day counter, and each structure is read at the instants they denote: QuantLib's grid runs from the risk-free curve's reference date and reads the dividend curve and the local volatility at its times. Where every structure shares the risk-free curve's day counter and reference date, QuantLib's reads and bits.
