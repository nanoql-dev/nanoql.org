Remarks

Most members assume that the cash flows are sorted by date, the redemptions after any cash flow on the same date; with a single redemption, it is the last cash flow. Prices are per 100 of the notional at the settlement date, as QuantLib's are: for a bond whose notional isn't 100, the cash price is the quote times the notional over 100, and the yield and Z-spread functions take their prices per 100 too.

Unsealed, an extension point, as QuantLib's concrete base: the fixed-rate, floating-rate, zero-coupon and amortizing bonds derive from it, and later bonds will. Its public constructors are QuantLib's two, which sort the cash flows they are given (stably: equal-dated cash flows keep their order) and check the issue date against the first payment. The construction contract is QuantLib's protected interface: a derived constructor calls the protected Bond(int, Calendar?, Date?), which builds no leg, then writes the protected cashflows, maturityDate and, for a bond with its own redemptions, redemptions, and calls the helpers that fill the notional schedule and the redemptions: AddRedemptionsToCashflows(IEnumerable<double>?), SetSingleRedemption(double, double, Date), SetSingleRedemption(double, CashFlow) or CalculateNotionalsFromCashflows(). That path keeps QuantLib's omissions: no issue-date check, and no sort of a leg already sorted. The protected state and the helpers are written while the bond is built, by a derived constructor, or on a fresh copy (WithCashflows(ImmutableArray<CashFlow>)), and never after: a convention the base can't enforce, since a derived class's every member can reach them, on which the bond's immutability rests.

The members that read the evaluation date, directly or through the settlement date or a coupon's fixing, take the context, QuantLib's default date becoming null: the settlement date, the notional, tradability, the accrued amount, the coupon rates, the cash-flow dates, and the prices and yields from a yield. The members that read QuantLib's attached engine (cleanPrice(), dirtyPrice(), settlementValue() and the theoretical yield) take that engine's results instead, with the context: they recompute the settlement date and the accrued amount from it, as QuantLib's recompute them from its one evaluation date, so the results and the context must be the same call's. Results read under another context mix two settlement dates, which nothing here can detect.

AddRedemptionsToCashflows(IEnumerable<double>?)

Builds the redemptions from the coupons' notionals and adds them to the cash flows: a derived constructor calls it after writing cashflows, which must hold at least one coupon and be sorted by date. It fills the notional schedule, the notionals and the redemptions.

protected void AddRedemptionsToCashflows(IEnumerable<double>? redemptions = null)

Parameters

redemptions IEnumerable<double>

The redemptions in base 100, multiplying each redemption's amount: 100 leaves it unchanged. Each notional step i from 1 takes element i, or the last element past the list's end, so element 0 is read only for a one-element list; empty by default, which redeems at 100.

Remarks

The redemptions are amortizing payments at each step but the last, a redemption at the last, each sorted after the coupons of its date by a stable sort, as QuantLib's std::stable_sort does.

Exceptions

NanoQLException

No cash flow is a coupon.

CalculateNotionalsFromCashflows()

Collects the notional schedule from the coupons, in the order of the cash flows: a new notional starts at each coupon whose contract nominal isn't close to the previous one's, and each ends at the last payment date of its coupons. It fills the notional schedule and the notionals, not the redemptions: a derived constructor calls it only when it provides its own redemptions, which it then writes into redemptions.

WithCashflows(ImmutableArray<CashFlow>)

A copy of the bond holding other cash flows: SetCouponPricer<T>(T, FloatingRateCouponPricer?)'s step, which QuantLib does in place on the coupons a built bond holds. The base's copy is memberwise, with the new leg, so the copy keeps the bond's type, terms, notional schedule and redemptions: the new leg must hold the same dates, nominals and redemption objects, as a leg with its pricers set does. A derived bond that keeps other state derived from its cash flows overrides it, and an override returns a copy of the same runtime type.
