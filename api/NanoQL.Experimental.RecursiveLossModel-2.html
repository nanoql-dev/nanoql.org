The recursive STCDO default loss model of a heterogeneous pool: names heterogeneous in their default probabilities, notionals and recoveries, their correlations given by the latent model. The recursive pricing algorithm is Andersen, Sidenius and Basu's, "All your hedges in one basket", Risk, November 2003, pages 67-72.

Remarks

With copulas other than the Gaussian it is only an approximation (see the remark on p. 68). The expected tranche loss inverts the sum and the integral (the first, unnumbered, equation on p. 70): EL(t) = ∫ q(ω) dω Σ_k l_k P(l_k; t | ω), the conditional expected loss integrated over the factor. The losses are counted in a loss unit, the smallest nonzero loss given default over the number of buckets, each name's loss given default rounded to a whole number of units, so the distribution's attainable losses are the sums of the names' weights. To be done, as in QuantLib: a loss unit from some small fraction of the portfolio's loss weights (fine for pricing, not for risk metrics; O'Kane 18.3.2), and integrands reading the inverted probabilities throughout.

The loss distribution labels each point by its attainable loss, and the percentile at 0 and 1 is the tranche's loss at the first and last, as its interior's are tranche losses: QuantLib's labels the i-th attainable loss i times the loss unit, wrong where the weights aren't 0, 1, 2, …, and its percentile returns the distribution's probability at both ends (1.0000000003 and 0.717; Divergences/RecursiveLossModelTests.cs). Bound to a basket, a copy computes the weights afresh, where QuantLib's appends them to the previous basket's, and prices the live names over the restated tranche. Refused, where QuantLib's reads before or past its vectors: a basket whose losses given default are all 0, and a percentile or shortfall whose search leaves the distribution. A shortfall the distribution never reaches is refused, as the binomial model's is, where QuantLib's reads 0 with its comment "well, we are in error.... fix: FAIL" (recursivelossmodel.hpp:298).
