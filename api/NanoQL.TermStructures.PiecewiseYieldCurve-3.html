Remarks

The curve bootstraps in its constructor, as a LazyObject's work is done at construction. While its bootstrap runs, the curve answers from a working state (BootstrapState<TTermStructure>), and its helpers are copies bound to the curve itself, as QuantLib binds them to this; when the bootstrap returns, the state is frozen into the curve's nodes and dropped, and the bound copies are discarded. Nothing outside the constructor sees the curve change. The helpers whose dates follow the evaluation date are re-dated for the context's (see BootstrapHelper<TTermStructure>). A curve in a cycle of curves, each bootstrapped with the others, is built by a MultiCurve.

QuantLib's curve derives from its traits' interpolated curve; this one is that curve through IInterpolatedCurve<TTraits, TInterpolator>, with the traits' formula and maximum-date rule. The members its bootstrap calls on the curve under construction (DiscountImpl(double), MaxDate, MaxTime, DayCounter) are sealed. Unsealed for its named classes, which add constructors only.

A spread curve (PiecewiseSpreadYieldCurve<TInterpolator, TBootstrap>) is bootstrapped on a base curve: its nodes are the spread discount factors, its discount the base's times theirs and its maximum date the earlier of its own and the base's, QuantLib's InterpolatedSpreadDiscountCurve; it allows extrapolation when the base does, when it is built. The base is read with extrapolation on, as the other derived curves read their originals. Every other curve has no base.

A curve is always built afresh: QuantLib's curve, recalculated after a quote or the evaluation date changed, starts from its previous state and, when its reference date is fixed, keeps its first evaluation date's pillars. Both are documented divergences.
