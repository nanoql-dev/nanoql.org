Remarks

QuantLib's curve derives from DerivedYieldTermStructure, a class template over its base, which C# can't express; it implements the template's members itself. Its day counter is the original's, read when it is built, and so is its extrapolation flag; its maximum date is the original's, read when it is asked for. QuantLib's constructor taking settlement days, whose reference date moves with the evaluation date, is Create(YieldTermStructure, int, Calendar, EvaluationContext). The original's time and discount at the reference date are read when the curve is built, where QuantLib reads them at the first discount; a curve whose original can't give them refuses to be built. An original that changes is a new curve: QuantLib's curve follows its handle.

A time on the curve is read from the original at the time plus the reference date's time on the original's clock, QuantLib's sum, which assumes year fractions add. A date is read by date where they don't: where the sum and the date's own time on the original's clock differ by more than 1e-12 relative, the curve's discount at a date is the original's ratio P(d)/P(ref) by date, the one QuantLib's formula states, and its zero rates and forward rates by date follow it. On Thirty360 and on Actual/Actual ISMA without a schedule QuantLib's sum misses that ratio, by up to 1.25e-4 and 3.7e-3 relative over four years of dates on a zero curve, and gives Thirty360's month-end dates of one time one discount; on every other date, and on the day counters whose year fractions add, the curve keeps QuantLib's bits. A date before the reference date, or past the maximum date unless extrapolating, is refused first, as a time is. A documented divergence.
