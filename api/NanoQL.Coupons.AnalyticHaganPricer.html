Remarks

Hagan's forms presume G = P(Tp)/A0, the payment's discount over the annuity, which the numeric pricer doesn't need, since it divides by G. QuantLib's shift models' G is P/A0, so their analytic convexity term is high by P/P(Tp) against the numeric twin; here it is QuantLib's term times P(Tp)/P, both on the forwarding curve. On an index with an exogenous discounting curve, where the annuity and discount come from that curve and the g-function from the forwarding one, every model's convexity term is normalised as the numeric pricer normalises its integrand, discount·G′/G for annuity·G′. The standard and exact-yield models on one curve keep QuantLib's bits.

So an index given a discounting curve equal to its forwarding one prices its analytic term up to the model's own approximation, 3e-4 of it, away from the same index without one.
