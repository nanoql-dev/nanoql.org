Remarks

An immutable object over its HestonModel and the integrations' accuracy and limit; the static Calculate builds one. The model's time runs from the earliest curve date, each curve read at the instant a time denotes; at the model's time 0 the price is the forward's discounted payoff, where QuantLib's window is zero-wide. The window is centred on the log forward, x₀ + ln(D_q/D_r), where QuantLib centres it on x₀ + ln(D_r/D_q), the forward's mirror, and misses the density at high carry and long expiry; its half-width 8√(θt + (v₀ − θ)(1 − e^{−κt})/κ) takes 1 − e^{−κt} by −expm1(−κt) below κt = 1e-2, where QuantLib's loses the (v₀ − θ) term.

The window misses the density's mass beyond it: on QuantLib's own markets, a half-year put at half the forward 3% to 53% low, and at a vol of variance of 1 and correlation −0.9 puts up to 18% low. nanoql adds the tails' values where they're worth more than the integrations' accuracy, QuantLib's price where they aren't: the payoff a line between its strikes, each piece's value is exact from the spot's distribution under the risk-neutral measure and under the share measure, a Heston distribution again, at the window's edges and the strikes; a payoff not linear on a tail with mass beyond the accuracy is refused. The value added is the additional result tailsValue (Divergences/AnalyticPdfHestonTailsTests.cs). A tail is left out where both its probabilities, under the two measures, are within the accuracy, the distribution's own noise there; such a tail can be worth up to (K + F) times the accuracy, so the tails' accuracy scales with the payoff, as the window's does.

Reference: A. Dragulescu, V. Yakovenko, Probability distribution of returns in the Heston model with stochastic volatility, 2002, http://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0203046.pdf.
