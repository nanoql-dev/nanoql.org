Remarks

If no payment convention is passed, the convention of the floating-rate schedule is used.

Abstract, as QuantLib's is, though it has no abstract member: QuantLib's setupFloatingArguments fills its engines' arguments, and nanoql's engines read the legs. A derived swap passes QuantLib's arguments and the builder of its floating leg to the protected constructor, which checks its terms and builds the fixed leg first and then calls the builder, in QuantLib's order: QuantLib's base constructor runs before the derived constructor's body builds the floating leg. The fixed leg is the first leg and the floating leg the second. An engine's results are a FixedVsFloatingSwapResults, whose fair rate and spread FetchResults(InstrumentResults) derives from the legs' BPS when the engine gives none.

When the context's IncludeReferenceDateEvents is true, payments occurring at the settlement date of the swap might be included in the NPV and therefore affect the fair-rate and fair-spread calculation. This might not be what you want.
