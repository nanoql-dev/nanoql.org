Remarks

Unsealed, as in QuantLib, where the presets (EuriborSwapIsdaFixA, ...) and OvernightIndexedSwapIndex derive from it. Its fixing is forecast from the swap UnderlyingSwap(Date, EvaluationContext) builds, priced by a DiscountingSwapEngine without the settlement date's flows: on the exogenous discounting curve when the index has one, and on the Ibor index's forwarding curve otherwise.

The constructor taking a discounting curve makes the index's discounting exogenous even when the curve is null, QuantLib's empty handle: a forecast then refuses "discounting term structure handle is empty", as QuantLib's does.

QuantLib caches the last swap built, keyed by its fixing date only, so a swap built under one at-par switch keeps serving forecasts after the switch changes. nanoql keeps no cache: each forecast builds its swap from the context it is given, and so reads the context's switch as a QuantLib index built afresh would.

Remarks

QuantLib builds the underlying swap, which reads the at-par switch, though the swap's dates don't depend on it: they come from its schedules. The swap is built here with an explicit coupon choice and its effective date, so its builder reads neither the switch nor the evaluation date, and the method takes no context.

Exceptions

ArgumentException

valueDate is default(Date), or its fixing date isn't a valid fixing date.
