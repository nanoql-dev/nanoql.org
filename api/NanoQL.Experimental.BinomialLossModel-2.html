The binomial defaultable basket loss model: the portfolio loss distribution approximated by an adjusted binomial, which fits the loss distribution's two moments and so allows the portfolio's inhomogeneity at little cost over the LHP. See Dominic O'Kane, "Approximating Independent Loss Distributions with an Adjusted Binomial Distribution", EDHEC Risk and Asset Management Research Centre, 2007, and "Modelling single name and multi-name credit derivatives", chapter 18.5.2, Wiley Finance, 2008.

Remarks

The version presented here is adapted to the multifactor case by computing a conditional binomial approximation; the binomial is stable, so the model serves risk management as well as pricing. The copula is arbitrary, though the model was designed for a Gaussian setup. QuantLib's template parameter is a loss latent model; the constant-loss model is the only one with the conditional recovery it reads, so it is the model's. To be done, as in QuantLib: the random recovery models, and the probability inversions the cumulative functions already computed.

The binomial's weight α mixes it with a two-point mass at the floor and ceiling of the expected count m, both of mean m, so the conditional variance matches the pool's: α = (V − V₂)/(V_B − V₂) with V the pool's count variance, V_B the binomial's and V₂ = (c − m)(m − f) the two-point mass's, all in QuantLib's units of a count over n². QuantLib takes V₂'s place with −(c − m)² − ((f − m)² − c²)(c − m), not a variance, so its conditional distribution misses the variance it exists to match, 10,656 for 11,718 on three names (Divergences/BinomialLossModelTests.cs); on a homogeneous pool V = V_B and α = 1, QuantLib's, taken where V equals V_B to 1e-12 in these units, since V_B − V₂ vanishes like the squared distance of the probability from 0 or 1, where the two variances' rounding differs most, and would move α (by 2e-7 at a distance of 1e-10); below that difference the variance a weight of 1 misses is negligible.

Bound to a basket with realized defaults, the model prices its live names over the restated tranche. The statistics read the notionals of the names alive at the evaluation date: QuantLib's read those alive at the statistic's date beside the evaluation date's recoveries, so a default programmed after the evaluation date reads past a vector, undefined; here it reads QuantLib's model without the event. A percentile or shortfall whose search leaves the loss distribution, which QuantLib reads before its start or past its end, is refused. The percentile at 1 is the tranche's loss at the largest attainable loss, as its interior's are tranche losses, where QuantLib's returns the pool's largest loss (binomiallossmodel.hpp:355).
