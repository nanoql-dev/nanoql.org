Andreasen and Huge's calibration of a local volatility surface to a sparse grid of European options: at each expiry a piecewise volatility in log strike, fitted so that one implicit step of the forward equation from the previous expiry's prices reprices the expiry's options; prices and local volatilities between expiries by partial steps. Andreasen J., Huge B., 2010. Volatility Interpolation, https://ssrn.com/abstract=1694972.

Remarks

Immutable, calibrated at construction (the playbook's LazyObject row), where QuantLib calibrates on first use. Its local-volatility and price slices at a time are memoised by that time, as QuantLib caches them, in thread-safe maps inside the object (a named departure from the stateless rule): each slice is computed from the calibration by QuantLib's arithmetic, so its bits are the same whichever thread computes it first, and every query returns what a fresh object returns. Without them an engine reading the surface at every grid point and step would recompute a slice per read, about 130 times QuantLib's cost.

The call-put calibration's error picks a put's error for a strike below the forward and a call's above it, where QuantLib compares the forward with the j-th grid point rather than the j-th strike, and so always takes the puts' (andreasenhugevolatilityinterpl.cpp:418). The dividend curve is read at the instants the risk-free curve's times denote, on its own clock, where QuantLib reads it at those times (the mismatched-dates class); curves that share a clock keep QuantLib's bits. Documented divergences.
