Remarks

QuantLib's curve derives from RelativeDerivedYieldTermStructure<> and from its interpolated curve; it implements the template's members itself. Its reference date and day counter are the base's, read when it is built, and so is its extrapolation flag; its calendar and settlement days are the base's, read when they are asked for. Its first node is at the base's reference date with a discount of 1; past its last node the spread extrapolates with a flat instantaneous forward rate, as InterpolatedDiscountCurve<TInterpolator> does, and its maximum date is the earlier of its last node's and the base's. A base that changes is a new curve: QuantLib's curve follows its handle. Unsealed for its named class, which adds constructors only. QuantLib's protected constructor, through which its piecewise spread curve derives, isn't ported: that curve is PiecewiseSpreadYieldCurve<TInterpolator, TBootstrap>.

QuantLib reads the base without extrapolation (spreaddiscountcurve.hpp:157), so asked to extrapolate past a base that doesn't, the curve throws where the zero- and forward-spreaded, implied and composite curves extrapolate; here the base is read with extrapolation on, as theirs are. A documented divergence.

Exceptions

ArgumentNullException

baseCurve, dates or dfs is null.

ArgumentException

There are fewer dates than the interpolation needs, the counts of dates and discounts differ, the first discount isn't 1, a discount isn't positive, the first date isn't the base's reference date, the dates aren't sorted or two of them give the same time, or the interpolation refuses the nodes.

NanoQLException

The interpolation refuses the nodes.
