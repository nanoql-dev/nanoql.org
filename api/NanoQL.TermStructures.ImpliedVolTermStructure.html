Remarks

Immutable over its original: QuantLib's structure follows its handle, and an original that changes is a new structure here. Its day counter, maximum date and strike range are the original's. As QuantLib warns, an implied structure makes financial sense only over an original that depends on time alone, not on the asset.

A time on the structure is read from the original between the reference date's time on the original's clock and that time plus the time, QuantLib's sum, which assumes year fractions add. A date is read by date where they don't: where the sum and the date's own time on the original's clock differ by more than 1e-12 relative, the structure's variance at a date is the original's forward variance from the reference date to that date by date, and its volatilities and forward variances by date follow it. On Thirty360 QuantLib's sum misses that variance, by +3.70% at 28 February and +0.75% at 30 June over a constant volatility; on every other date, and on the day counters whose year fractions add, the structure keeps QuantLib's bits. A reference date before the original's accrues no variance before the original's, where QuantLib's read counts the original's variance at a negative time (the mismatched-dates class). A documented divergence.
