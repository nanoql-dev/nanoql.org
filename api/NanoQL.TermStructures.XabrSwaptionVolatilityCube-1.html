Remarks

The cube fits the model's smile to the market volatilities, the at-the-money volatility plus each spread, at each node of its grid (the sparse parameters). An at-the-money-calibrated cube then fills a dense grid, the union of its grid and its at-the-money structure's: off its own nodes, a strike's volatility is the at-the-money volatility plus the spread interpolated between the four surrounding nodes' fitted smiles at the strike of the same strike spread (a normal cube) or moneyness (a lognormal one), and it fits the smile there again (the dense parameters). In a single pass, the dense grid's spreads are the quoted ones interpolated the same way and the dense fit is the only one; with at-the-money calibration each node's α is then solved so that the section returns the at-the-money volatility, and the node's errors are recomputed at that α. Each fit starts from the parameter guesses interpolated at its node, and restarts as its model's fit does. A node whose fit ends on its iteration limit, or whose error reaches the tolerance, is refused with QuantLib's text.

A query's smile section is the model's section at the parameters and forward interpolated at its option time and swap length, bilinearly between the nodes or, with backward-flat interpolation, backward flat in option time and linear in swap length, both flat outside the grid. It reads only what the cube stores, not the context.

QuantLib's cube calibrates on its first query, and recalibrates when its quotes, its structures or the evaluation date change; this one calibrates when it is built, under the context it is given, so a refusal of the calibration comes at construction, and a new market is a new cube. The parameter guesses are read on the cube's grid, its at-the-money structure's, where QuantLib reads them on the grid of its construction-time reference date, the evaluation date: the two differ where the structure's reference date isn't that date. Immutable and thread-safe once built.

QuantLib's recalibration members change a calibrated cube: they set a swap tenor's β guesses and refit that tenor's sections, and updateAfterRecalibration then refits the dense grid. Here each Recalibration(IEnumerable<double>, Period, EvaluationContext) overload returns a new cube, a copy of this one's calibrated state with both steps applied, and leaves this cube unchanged. The β values are the fits' guesses, so they are the sections' βs where the cube fixes β and the refit's starting point where it doesn't.

An extension point, as in QuantLib: a model of one's own is a TModel, and QuantLib-Python's named cubes derive from it, as SabrSwaptionVolatilityCube does.

Whether each model parameter is fixed at its guess; flags past the model's parameters are ignored.

Whether the cube fills and fits its dense grid.

The fits' end criteria, or null for the model fit's default.

The fits' optimizer, or null for the model fit's default.

errorAccept double?

The error at or below which a fit is accepted and its restarts stop, or null for a fifth of the tolerance.

useMaxError bool

Whether a fit is scored by its maximum error, not its root-mean-square error.

maxGuesses int

backwardFlat bool

singlePassCalibration bool

Exceptions

ArgumentNullException

context, the structure, a collection or an index is null.

ArgumentException

The cube's base refuses its arguments; there are fewer than 2 option or swap tenors; the guesses' rows aren't one per option and swap tenor pair, or a row has fewer guesses than the model's parameters; there are fewer fixed flags than the model's parameters; the at-the-money structure isn't a SwaptionVolatilityDiscrete for a cube that fills its dense grid; or a fit refuses its points.

ArgumentOutOfRangeException

maxGuesses is negative.

NanoQLException

A fit fails or reaches the tolerance, an at-the-money strike can't be computed, or the dense grid reaches past the cube's.
