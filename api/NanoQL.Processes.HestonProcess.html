The Heston square-root stochastic-volatility process: dS = μS dt + √v S dW₁, dv = κ(θ − v) dt + σ√v dW₂, with dW₁dW₂ = ρ dt. Its state is the underlying and the variance.

Remarks

Immutable: the curves, the spot and the parameters are fixed at construction, where QuantLib's process holds handles and a quote. Evolve(double, Vector, double, Vector) steps by the chosen HestonProcessDiscretization; the exact schemes sample the variance from its non-central chi-squared law, whose inverse refuses where its series doesn't converge, and Broadie and Kaya's schemes also sample the integrated variance.

An extension point, as in QuantLib: BatesProcess derives from it. The in-place Evolve(double, ReadOnlySpan<double>, double, ReadOnlySpan<double>, Span<double>) steps the truncation, reflection and quadratic-exponential schemes without allocating, by QuantLib's arithmetic, its rates read without building QuantLib's rate objects, so its bits are the allocating form's; the exact schemes' samplers allocate, and step through the allocating form.

Pdf(double, double, double, double)

The joint density of the log spot x and the variance v at time t: the variance's non-central chi-squared density times the log spot's conditional density given it, from the integrated variance's characteristic function (Broadie and Kaya).

public double Pdf(double x, double v, double t, double eps = 0.001)

Parameters

x double

The log spot.

v double

The variance.

t double

The time, on the risk-free curve's clock.

eps double

QuantLib's accuracy for a tail search whose result its own code overwrites (hestonprocess.cpp:307-323): the density depends on it neither there nor here.

Returns

double

The density.

Remarks

The dividend curve is read at the instant t denotes on the risk-free curve's clock, nothing accrued before its own reference date (the mismatched-dates class); QuantLib's read, the dividend curve's own time t, where the two curves share their times.

QuantLib's density doesn't hold its digits (hestonprocess.cpp:300-334). It integrates over the integrated variance up to twice a Cornish-Fisher quantile whose skew and kurtosis are finite differences of the characteristic function at a step of 1e-2, which rest on its last bits, and that cut-off can fall where the integrand is still a fifth of its peak (10% low on ordinary inputs); and its 128-point Gauss-Laguerre rule for the conditional density reaches u ≈ 500, where at short times the characteristic function hasn't decayed (NaN and negative densities at a quarter of a year). Here the log spot's conditional density given the variance is the inversion of its characteristic function: given the integrated variance ξ the log spot is normal, with mean x₀ + a − cξ and variance (1 − ρ²)ξ, c = ½ − ρκ/σ, as QuantLib's integrand has it, so its characteristic function given the variance is e^{iu(x₀ + a)}·Φ(−uc + i·½u²(1 − ρ²)), Φ the integrated variance's, with the Bessel ratio in its exponentially weighted form. The inversion integral runs by 32-point Gauss-Legendre panels no wider than the integrand's oscillation and decay allow, out to where E[e^{−½u²(1 − ρ²)ξ}], which bounds the integrand, falls below 1e-17. A documented divergence (Divergences/HestonPdfTests.cs), checked by the log spot's marginal, the density integrated over the variance, against the log spot's density from Heston's characteristic function, which QuantLib's analytic Heston engine prices by. Where the variance's density is 0 the density is 0. The value's absolute accuracy is about 1e-13, the inversion's rounding floor: far from the density's mass it is noise of that size, of either sign.

Exceptions

NanoQLException

Boost's non-central chi-squared refuses its arguments; |ρ| is 1, where the log spot's conditional density has no variance; or the inversion doesn't converge within 100,000 panels.
