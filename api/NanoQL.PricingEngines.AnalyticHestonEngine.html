Remarks

Two algebraically equivalent formulations of the characteristic function's complex logarithm exist. Gatheral's (also Duffie, Pan and Singleton's, and Schoutens, Simons and Tistaert's) has no discontinuities and works with adaptive integrations; Heston's original needs a branch correction, which refuses them. The control-variate forms, Andersen and Piterbarg's, the asymptotic expansion's and the angled contour's, integrate the difference from a value they price in closed form.

Immutable: it holds the model and the integration settings. Each price returns its number of evaluations with it (PriceVanillaPayoffWithStatistics(PlainVanillaPayoff, Date), and the results' additional result "numberOfEvaluations"), where QuantLib keeps the last call's count on the engine. The static Calculate overloads build an engine with the given settings and price through it.

An extension point, as in QuantLib: jump-diffusion engines such as Bates's derive from it and add their term to the characteristic function through AddOnTerm(double, double, int).

A price at a maturity date runs the model's time on the risk-free curve's day counter from the earliest reference date of the model's curves, each accruing nothing before its own, so the variance accrues from the evaluation date beside a risk-free curve settling later, as QuantLib's engine on the market restated from that date prices it; QuantLib's time runs from the risk-free curve's date, so its reads throw before that date and its integrations read 0/0 on it. An option expiring at the model's time 0, on or before every curve's date, is priced at the forward's intrinsic value. Where the dividend curve isn't dated before the risk-free curve, the time is QuantLib's. A documented divergence (Divergences/ExerciseBeforeReferenceDateTests.cs, Divergences/HestonMismatchedTimesTests.cs).
