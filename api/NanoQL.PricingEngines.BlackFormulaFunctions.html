Remarks

The Black formulas take a standard deviation, the volatility times the square root of the time to maturity, instead of a volatility; the Bachelier formulas take an absolute (normal) volatility, not a percentage one.

Each payoff overload refuses a null payoff, where QuantLib dereferences it, and passes the payoff's option type and strike on: a refusal of the strike names strike, as the overload it calls raises it. QuantLib's checks run in QuantLib's order, after the checks nanoql adds on entry (an option type the formula computes with, a negative maxIterations), with QuantLib's texts.

BlackFormula(OptionType, double, double, double, double, double)

Black's 1976 formula: the price of an option on a forward, with a shifted lognormal volatility when a displacement is given.

public static double BlackFormula(OptionType optionType, double strike, double forward, double stdDev, double discount = 1, double displacement = 0)

Parameters

optionType OptionType

Call or put.

strike double

The strike.

forward double

The forward.

stdDev double

The standard deviation, the volatility times the square root of the time.

discount double

The discount factor.

displacement double

The displacement of the shifted lognormal model.

Returns

double

The price.

Remarks

A negative result within 16·ε·D·max(F, K) of zero, D the discount and F and K the forward and the strike displaced, is the formula's rounding residue and returns 0, where QuantLib's check refuses it ("negative value"): the price is never negative on the inputs the formula accepts, and the residue is at most 5.875·ε·D·max(F, K), where its two arguments straddle the normal distribution's switch to its asymptotic series (Divergences/BlackFormulaResidueTests.cs). A larger negative, a negative where the bound's scale 16·ε·D·max(F, K) overflows, and NaN still refuse, with QuantLib's text.

Parameters

strike double

The strike.
