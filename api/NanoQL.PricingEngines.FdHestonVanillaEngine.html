The finite-difference Heston pricing engine for vanilla options, European, American or Bermudan, with cash dividends, a quanto adjustment and a leverage function optional: FdmHestonSolver on FdmBlackScholesMesher's grid of the log spot, sized by the variance mesher's volatility estimate, by FdmHestonLocalVolatilityVarianceMesher's grid of the variance.

Remarks

The engine reports the value, delta, gamma and theta, the theta null when the problem's first stopping time is time 0. Its finite-difference working types are built inside each calculation and dropped after it. Its times run on Clock(HestonProcess, FdmQuantoHelper?, LocalVolTermStructure?), from the earliest reference date of the structures it reads, each accruing nothing before its own date, where QuantLib's run from the risk-free curve's; where none is dated before the risk-free curve, they are QuantLib's (Divergences/FdHestonMismatchedTimesTests.cs). The variance mesher's skew factor is bounded and its fallbacks are master's.

QuantLib sizes the equity mesher on a helper process whose curves its call binds in each other's places, so the mesher walks the forward's mirror about the spot; where the carry is large against the volatility the grid's far edge falls inside the distribution and the price stops converging (a five-year call at 1.25 times the forward, carry 0.30 and volatility 0.1, priced 0 where the analytic engine gives 1.5732), and where cash dividends take the mirrored walk below zero its bound isn't a number and the engine refuses. Here the mesher is QuantLib's wherever its range holds the true forwards' band, the walk's ends on the helper's curves in place widened by the volatility's quantile, half the mesher's own margin, and the strike widened by the same half margin, and QuantLib's mesher widened to the band through its own constraints where it doesn't, a bound that isn't a number falling short. The multi-strike solve, whose mesher takes no constraints, solves each strike alone where its grid doesn't hold the band at the points the solve is read at, S·K/Kᵢ, their forwards quanto-adjusted where a quanto helper is given; the two paths run on different grids, so a strike's value steps where the switch moves, by up to about 0.4% at carry 0.06 (Divergences/FdHestonHighCarryTests.cs).

QuantLib's enableMultipleStrikesCaching solves once on a mesher spanning several strikes and serves later options at those strikes from the solve, each V(S·d)/d with d = K/Kᵢ; the strike a later call is served depends on which option solved first, which a cache may not do here: a value doesn't depend on what was computed before it. Here CalculateStrikes(VanillaOption, IEnumerable<double>, EvaluationContext, HestonModel, int, int, int, int, FdmSchemeDesc?, LocalVolTermStructure?, double) returns every strike's results from one solve on the given option, each QuantLib's cached value bit for bit. Where the scaling doesn't hold, QuantLib's cache serves values far from the strike's own price: with a leverage function, which a spot-dependent leverage breaks (at 125, 0.830 cached where the direct price is 0.433), and after a reference whose payoff isn't a plain vanilla (6.819 served for a plain call at 80 after a cash-or-nothing reference, its direct price 22.945); there each strike's plain vanilla is solved alone, QuantLib's engine priced for that strike (Divergences/FdHestonMultipleStrikesLeverageTests.cs, Divergences/FdHestonStrikesReferenceTests.cs).
