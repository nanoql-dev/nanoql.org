Remarks

Its short rate is f⁻¹(xₜ + g(t)), xₜ a GeneralizedOrnsteinUhlenbeckProcess and g the deterministic fitting parameter, which can't be determined analytically: a tree fits it to the curve at each step as it is built, by Brent (2,000 evaluations, accuracy 1e-8, in [−50, 50] from the previous step's value), each tree its own fitting, as QuantLib's. Its parameters are α's values at its speed dates and σ's at its volatility dates, in that order, interpolated over their times from the curve's reference date (InterpolationParameter).

A, B and the bond options are Hull-White's with time-dependent parameters (Gurrieri, Nakabayashi and Wong, 2009), on QuantLib's numerical integrals of α and σ, and valid only where f is the identity: at a constant α and σ they are within those integrals' error of HullWhite's closed forms (4.6e-4 relative on a bond option at α 0.1), QuantLib's design, kept.

Dynamics() refuses, as QuantLib's does: the general model has no process, and HwDynamics() gives Hull-White's at α(0) and σ(0), with the analytical fitting parameter φ(t) = f(t) + ½[σ(1 − e^(−αt))/α]², f the instantaneous forward. Below |α| 1e-2 φ is in its cancellation-free form through expm1, σt at α = 0: QuantLib cuts to σt below α √ε, which jumps there and cancels just above, and takes that cut for every negative α (φ(5) 3.6e-4 low at −0.05) (Divergences/GeneralizedHullWhiteSmallReversionTests.cs).
