Remarks

QuantLib's template on the tree and the discretized asset: Calculate<TTree, TAsset>(DoubleBarrierOption, EvaluationContext, GeneralizedBlackScholesProcess, int) takes both as type arguments, the tree built through Create(StochasticProcess1D, double, int, double) and the asset through Create(DoubleBarrierOption, StochasticProcess, TimeGrid?), DiscretizedDoubleBarrierOption (QuantLib's default), DiscretizedDermanKaniDoubleBarrierOption or a user's own. The delta and gamma come from the lattice's first two steps, the gamma and delta null where the nodes don't separate, and the theta from the second step's middle node, as QuantLib's. As QuantLib's, the engine takes a spot beyond a barrier to the lattice, which knocks it at its first node.

Documented divergences. The tree runs on the risk-free curve's day counter from the earliest reference date of the structures, each structure on another day counter or reference date flattened on it from its discount or variance at the maturity date, and the asset's exercise times read on the tree's clock, as BinomialBarrierEngine, where QuantLib flattens each curve from the risk-free curve's date on its own day counter (Divergences/BinomialDoubleBarrierMismatchedTimesTests.cs); where every structure shares the risk-free curve's day counter and reference date, the tree is QuantLib's. The time grid and the lattice take their step count from the tree, its columns less one, where QuantLib's take the count given: the Leisen-Reimer and Joshi trees build on one more step at an even count.
