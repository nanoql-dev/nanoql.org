Remarks

The source's arbitrage-free region (SmileSectionUtils) is its core. Left of it the call prices are a Black call plus a constant, right of it a Black call or, with exponential extrapolation, an exponential; inside it the source's prices are kept, or, with interpolation, replaced by Kahale's segment functions, one per grid interval. So in the leftmost interval and right of the last grid point the source's smile is always replaced. A shifted source is handled by shifting its forward and strikes and fitting as for a lognormal one; a normal source is refused. The fit runs at construction.

Five of QuantLib's defects are fixed. The put branch of the volatility implies it from the call at the shifted strike, where QuantLib's adds the unshifted one (kahalesmilesection.cpp:255), so with a shift every put-branch volatility reads 0. The left wing's check reads a digital at the wing's midpoint, (k₁ − 2·shift)/2 unshifted for the wing's shifted end k₁, where QuantLib's reads it at (k₁ − shift)/2 (:109-110), which lies in the core once its left end is negative unshifted, so the check fails and the core moves right; with interpolation it also reads a segment function not yet fitted, undefined behaviour. A segment that fails to fit removes the unfitted slot after its own, where QuantLib's removes the slot at the grid index after the segment's (:163), past the slots' end once the core's left index is 2 or more, undefined behaviour; every slot either removes is unfitted, so the two agree wherever QuantLib's is in range. And AtmLevel() is the given or the source's level, where QuantLib's returns it shifted (kahalesmilesection.hpp:142, .cpp:59), so with a shift its vega and its converted volatilities read the forward plus the shift. And a segment whose fitted function's forward runs far above its strikes, to 10¹⁴ and more, so that QuantLib's Black call and constant cancel and its prices are the cancellation's, even below their intrinsic value (:150-173), is solved again, and evaluated, anchored at its left end's price, the Black call's change taken through complementary normal integrals; a segment whose terms round within 1e-8 of its ends' prices, relative, is QuantLib's, solved and evaluated as QuantLib's, at any scale; one the anchored solve can't fit fails as QuantLib's unsolved one, its right point removed.

And with interpolation, a segment whose fit misses its right end's price is refitted (Divergences/KahaleMissedSegmentTests.cs). QuantLib's fit solves for the segment's slope a by a Brent search (kahalesmilesection.cpp:151-155), but a forms the law's tails at the segment's ends, N(d₂(k₀)) = a − c′₀ and N(d₂(k₁)) = a − c′₁, by subtraction, so where the root's lognormal law puts less than about 1e-16 of its mass beyond an end, adjacent doubles of a skip over the root and the search's bracket closes on a non-root (brent.hpp:87-90). The segment then misses its right end's price and the section jumps there, an arbitrage: on a lognormal SABR smile (a forward of 5% for a year, α 0.3, β 1, ν 0.3, ρ −0.3) QuantLib's call a millionth of the forward below a strike of 7.5% is 0.000326 against 0.000646 at it. A fitted segment that misses its right end by more than 1e-8 of the price, QuantLib's own test's tolerance, and its evaluation's rounding is solved again to the same ends and slopes on the law's tail parameter u, its mass below k₀ (1 − Δ)·σ(u) and above k₁ (1 − Δ)·σ(−u), Δ = c′₁ − c′₀ and σ the logistic function, so that every member of the family is reachable, and evaluated without the law's forward, its first moment on the segment through Mills' ratios where the forward would overflow. A refit keeps QuantLib's ends and slopes, so nothing else in the section moves; a segment no refit fits fails as QuantLib's unsolved one, its right point removed. Without interpolation, the default, no segment is fitted and the core keeps the source's prices.

Two of QuantLib's limits are kept. Where a segment's solve fails, its right point is removed, as QuantLib's is, which can leave one segment over most of the core (on the smile above, from 1% to 150% of the forward) far from the source; the same refit would fit most such segments, but a rescued segment far out in a wing carries QuantLib's averaged-secant slopes over a wide interval and can stray from the source further than QuantLib's Black wing does. And the left wing's fit can run its forward to 10⁵ to 10⁷ times the section's, its constant near minus that, so its prices step by an ulp of that forward, a few calls falling below their intrinsic value by about 1e-11; anchoring it at its right end would move the core through QuantLib's wing check, which reads the wing's digital.

QuantLib's public helper structs (cFunction and the three solvers' functions) are the fit's internals here.
