Doust's no-arbitrage SABR model (Paul Doust, "No-arbitrage SABR", The Journal of Computational Finance, 2012): the SABR density with the absorption at zero read off a simulated table, its option and digital prices the density's integrals, the forward adjusted so the density reprices it.

Remarks

Immutable: QuantLib's forward solve, which changes its forward and normalization as it searches, runs over a working state at construction, the found forward and normalization stored. The model's domain is QuantLib's: an expiry in (0, 30], β in [0.01, 0.99], σ_I = αF^(β − 1) in [0.05, 1], ν in [0.01, 0.8], ρ in [−0.99, 0.99]. As QuantLib's header says, the forward adjustment can fail at extreme parameters, which Forward against NumericalForward shows: at an expiry of 30, ρ 0.9, ν 0.8 the adjustment is 0.0157 off and the call flat in strike; at β 0.9, ν 0.8, |ρ| 0.7 the density beyond QuantLib's integration domain carries a strike-independent call value, 4.0e-6 at an expiry of 1 (σ_I 0.3) and 3.5e-4 at 4 (σ_I 0.05). Out-of-the-money prices below the integrator's 1e-7 carry no relative accuracy, so implied volatilities read there are unreliable.

Documented divergence (Divergences/NoArbSabrShortExpiryTests.cs): where σ_I√T is below 0.1, each of the model's integrals (the normalization and the price inside the forward solve, the price, the digital) is split at the density's peak, the current forward ± 8w with w = Fσ_I√T, where QuantLib's single interval misses the spike: out of the money from T 0.005, at the money from 0.003, the forward adjustment failing at 0.001; at and above 0.1 QuantLib's single interval and bits stand.
