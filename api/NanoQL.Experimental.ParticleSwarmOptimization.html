Remarks

M particles explore the N-dimensional parameter space. Each position moves by its velocity, X_{i,j}^{k+1} = X_{i,j}^k + V_{i,j}^{k+1}, the velocity V_{i,j}^{k+1} = χ(V_{i,j}^k + c₁ r_{i,j}^k (P_{i,j}^k − X_{i,j}^k) + c₂ R_{i,j}^k (G_{i,j}^k − X_{i,j}^k)), with r and R uniform in [0, 1], P the particle's personal best and G its social best, which the topology sets: the PSO with constriction factor (PSO-Co). The PSO with inertia factor (PSO-In) takes ω, ĉ₁ and ĉ₂ in place of χ, χc₁ and χc₂. χ is 2/|2 − φ − √(φ² − 4φ)| with φ = c₁ + c₂; stable convergence needs φ ≥ 4, and Clerc and Kennedy recommend c₁ = c₂ = 2.05. The inertia scales the previous velocity, a constant in the canonical PSO. The optimization stops at its iterations or after its stationary iterations; positions are clamped to the bounds, the velocity zeroed there.

The swarm starts on a Sobol sequence within the bounds, its seed the optimizer's: QuantLib's is 0, the clock, read only past the 32 dimensions of Jaeckel's direction integers, so the bits are QuantLib's up to 16 parameters. The velocities' uniforms are a Mersenne Twister's on the seed, which is required: QuantLib's default seeds from the clock, and its two constructors are ambiguous for an integer literal there. A problem's constraint is never empty, so QuantLib's refusal of one doesn't arise. Immutable: each minimization copies the topology and the inertia and starts the generators from the seed, where QuantLib's continues them and appends a new swarm to the last.
