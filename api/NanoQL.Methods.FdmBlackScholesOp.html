The Black-Scholes operator in the logarithm of the underlying, in one direction: the drift r − q − σ²/2 on the first derivative, half the variance on the second and the discounting −r, each over a time step, the variance the Black forward variance or, on request, the local variance at each point.

Remarks

A working type: SetTime(double, double) rewrites the operator's bands in place, through buffers it holds, by QuantLib's arithmetic in QuantLib's order, so a time step allocates nothing where QuantLib builds new bands and arrays. The forward rates come from the curves' compound factors as QuantLib's forwardRate computes them, without building the rate object.

The finite-difference engine's times are the risk-free curve's, measured from its reference date in its day counter (blackscholesprocess.cpp:149-152), and QuantLib reads the dividend curve, the volatility and the quanto helper's structures at those times (fdmblackscholesop.cpp:51-66), each of which measures time from its own reference date in its own day counter: a one-year put on a risk-free curve on Act/360 prices 0.93% above the same market restated on one day counter, and one on a risk-free curve settling two business days after its dividend curve's and volatility's date 0.74% below QuantLib's analytic engine, which counts each structure from its own date. Here the engine's times, the ones SetTime(double, double) takes, run on the risk-free curve's day counter from the earliest reference date of the structures it reads (Clock(GeneralizedBlackScholesProcess, FdmQuantoHelper?)), and each structure, the risk-free curve among them, is read at the instants the step's times denote, on its own clock, accruing nothing before its own reference date, as the analytic engine counts it: its forward rate or variance between them per unit of the engine's time, the local volatility at the step's midpoint scaled to that time. Between Actual/N day counters the instants are exact, a reference date's offset a constant shift; on others a time is read through its date, linear within the day. Where no structure is dated before the risk-free curve, the times are its own, and a structure that shares its day counter and reference date is read as QuantLib reads it. A documented divergence.

Clock(GeneralizedBlackScholesProcess, FdmQuantoHelper?)

The finite-difference Black-Scholes engine's clock: the times its mesher, this operator, the solver's maturity, the step conditions and the escrowed dividend adjustment run on. Its day counter is the risk-free curve's and its reference date the earliest of the structures the engine reads, the process's curves and volatilities and the quanto helper's, so the days before a later-dated structure's date are time on the grid, each structure accruing nothing before its own date. Where no structure is dated before the risk-free curve, the clock is the risk-free curve itself, whose times are the process's.

Remarks

A solver assembled from these types, as QuantLib's other finite-difference engines assemble theirs, takes its maturity and its other times from the clock's TimeFromReference(Date), and the step conditions' reference date and day counter from the clock, where QuantLib takes them from the process. Its only use is its times.
