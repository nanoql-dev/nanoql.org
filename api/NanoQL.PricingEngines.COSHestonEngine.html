Remarks

An immutable object over its HestonModel, the truncation range's width and the series' length; the static Calculate(VanillaOption, EvaluationContext, HestonModel, double, int) builds one and prices through it. The model's time runs from the earliest curve date and the forward and discount are read by date (AnalyticHestonEngine's reads); at the model's time 0 the price is QuantLib's bound, the forward's discounted intrinsic value, before any cumulant.

The cumulants are QuantLib's expressions, evaluated where κt calls for it in another arithmetic: QuantLib's double, and so its bits, from each cumulant's cut to its upper cut; double-double below the cut, quad-double below double-double's floor and, below quad-double's, each cumulant's cubic in κ through four quad-double values; past the upper cut, where e^{nκt} overflows, an extended-range double, each cut measured at the run against QuantLib's expressions in 50 to 250 digits. At a term of 0 the cumulants, the skew and the kurtosis are their limits, 0. Below σ = NanoQL.PricingEngines.COSHestonEngine.SigmaCut, where the classic form's division by σ² cancels, the characteristic function is ChF(Complex, double)'s, the same function in Andersen and Lake's form.

The series' length has to grow with the vol of variance and the term: at QuantLib's defaults, a range of 16 and 200 terms, a vol of variance of 1 prices a five-year call at twice the forward at −7.8 times its value and a twenty-year one 39% low, where 2,000 terms bring them within 0.6% and 0.02%; QuantLib's own large-σ case takes a range of 25 and 600 terms.

References: F. Fang, C.W. Oosterlee, A Novel Pricing Method for European Options based on Fourier-Cosine Series Expansions; F. Le Floc'h, Fourier Integration and Stochastic Volatility Calibration.
