Remarks

QuantLib's curve derives from RelativeDerivedYieldTermStructure<ZeroYieldStructure>, a class template over its own base, which C# can't express; it implements the template's members itself, as ZeroSpreadedTermStructure does. Its reference date and day counter are the original's, read when it is built, and so is its extrapolation flag; its calendar and settlement days are the original's, read when they are asked for, and its maximum date is the earlier of the original's and the last spread's date. The spreads' times are counted from the reference date when the curve is built. Spreads or an original that change make a new curve: QuantLib's curve follows its quotes and its handle. QuantLib's deprecated constructor taking a day counter, which it ignores, isn't ported.

The zero rate at a time is the original's, continuously compounded, plus the spread's average from the reference date to that time. QuantLib integrates the interpolation from the first spread's time (piecewiseforwardspreadedtermstructure.hpp:152-166), leaving out the flat spread it applies itself before that time, and reads the interpolation extrapolated backwards before it: a 1% spread from year 1 over a flat 2% curve gives zero rates of 1.0% at six months and 2.75% at four years, where the curve's own forwards are 3% throughout. Here the spread is integrated from the reference date, flat before the first spread's time, so the zero rates are the forwards' averages; with the first spread at the reference date it is QuantLib's integral and bits. A documented divergence.
