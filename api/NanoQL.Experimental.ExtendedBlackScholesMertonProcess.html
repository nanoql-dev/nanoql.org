Remarks

The diffusion is the Black volatility at the time and the asset value, extrapolated, and the drift the curves' forward rates over the next 1e-4 of a year less half its square, as QuantLib's. The step is the scheme's, in the log price.

Three fixes (Divergences/ExtendedBlackScholesProcessTests.cs). Milstein's correction is the log price's, ½σ·∂σ/∂ln S·(ΔW² − 1)Δt, ∂σ/∂ln S the Black volatility's central difference over a log strike of ±1e-4, 0 on a strike-independent volatility, where the step is the log-Euler one; QuantLib adds the asset's own, ½σ²(ΔW² − 1)Δt, to the log price, its mean 1.25e-3 high a step at Δt 0.25 and σ 0.4. Euler's step, and the predictor-corrector's predictor, are the log-Euler step on the process's drift and diffusion; QuantLib's centre on the asset's mean, x₀e^((r−q)Δt), which lacks the −σ²Δt/2 (2.02e-2 high a step at Δt 0.25 and σ 0.4) and refuses a strike-dependent volatility ("not implemented"). The corrector is QuantLib's on a strike-independent volatility. On one that depends on the strike, QuantLib's corrector, its diffusion the mean of the two ends' on the same increment, is Heun's scheme, which converges to the Stratonovich equation, ½σ·∂σ/∂ln S above the log drift: on a SABR smile its error stops falling with the step, E[S_T] 0.09% low; nanoql's takes each end's drift less ½σ·∂σ/∂ln S, the Itô predictor-corrector, which converges at order 1 there and keeps QuantLib's bits where ∂σ/∂ln S is 0. On flat curves and a flat volatility all three are then the exact step, and on a smile they converge to the process at their orders, Euler's ½ and the others' 1.
