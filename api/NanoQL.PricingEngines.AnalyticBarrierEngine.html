Remarks

QuantLib's formulas, with four changes, each a documented divergence that leaves QuantLib's bits where it doesn't apply. At a variance below machine epsilon the underlying follows its forward, as QuantLib's master prices it (e29c64126): a knock-out pays its rebate at the hit or the vanilla payoff, a knock-in the vanilla payoff or its rebate at expiry, a spot on the barrier a hit. A negative rebate is priced, where QuantLib's E and F test rebate > 0 and drop it. Each rate is read on the volatility's clock, the curve's zero rate times its own time over the volatility's, nothing accrued before a structure's reference date, so curves on other day counters or dates price as QuantLib's engine does on the same market restated on the volatility's (a factor of 1.0, and QuantLib's bits, where the clocks agree; 1.0 where the volatility's time is 0). A knock-out's rebate whose closed form has no real λ (λ² = μ² + 2r/σ² < 0, at negative rates), where QuantLib's is NaN, is the rebate's present value at the hit, integrated over the barrier's first-passage density.

The formulas are first-passage closed forms in one equivalent-constant carry and volatility, the variance and discounts to expiry: no exact closed form exists for a first passage under general deterministic term structures, and on shaped markets they miss QuantLib's finite-difference barrier engine by -2.4% to +0.6%. They are exact where (r − q)/σ² is constant over the option's life and the volatility depends on time alone, the price then a time change of a constant market's, so a smile breaks it; a knock-out's rebate, paid at the hit, where r/σ² and q/σ² are each constant (Conventions/ClosedFormExactnessTests.cs). The term-structure-exact route is FdBlackScholesBarrierEngine; the Monte Carlo engines monitor the barrier at their grid's dates.

QuantLib's references: Haug's formulas, and its testHaugValues table.
