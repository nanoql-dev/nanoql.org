A mesher for an exponential jump process with high mean reversion and low jump intensity, dY = −βY dt + J dN, the jumps exponential of mean 1/η (Hambly, Howison and Kluge, "Modelling spikes and pricing swing options in electricity markets"): its points at the stationary jump-size distribution's quantiles, to 1 − ε.

Remarks

Immutable. The distribution at a finite time, JumpSizeDistribution(double, double), is the distribution of the last jump's decayed size, ∫₀ᵗ λe^(−λu)/(1 − e^(−λt))·(1 − e^(−ηx·e^(βu))) du, integrated over u and split where ηx·e^(βu) crosses 1, or as 1 less its complement's integral where ηx is at least 1. QuantLib integrates its density over x from 1e-100 instead, which holds no mass while βt is below about 230; beyond, it misses mass (1.06e-4 at βt 500) and from βt 800 its integrator refuses (a documented divergence). Where its cut holds no mass the two meet within 4.4e-13, each within 4.5e-13 absolute of a 40-digit evaluation, the tolerance of both integrals being absolute; at an intensity at or above β, where QuantLib's density at t refuses, the route computes. Both routes take 1 − e^(−λt) by expm1, which QuantLib's subtraction loses at small λt.

The density at a finite time, JumpSizeDensity(double, double), is the route's derivative, integrated by tanh-sinh to a relative tolerance and split at the same point. QuantLib's closed form, Γ(1 − λ/β)·(P(1 − λ/β, ηx·e^(βt)) − P(1 − λ/β, ηx)) over its other factors, cancels as βt falls (9.9e-5 relative at β 10, λ 2, η 1, t 1e-12) and where ηx is large, both gammas near 1 (1.1e-7 relative at x 20, βt 0.01; 0 for 4e-20 to 7e-18 where they underflow), and refuses at an intensity at or above β (its Γ's argument) and from βt about 750; it is replaced (a fix, a documented divergence), within 5e-12 relative of a 40-digit evaluation (tanh-sinh's √ε), where the value is in the double range: it is refused where it leaves it at x 0 (e^((β − λ)t) beyond it), 0 below it, and at subnormal sizes, where an integrand value leaves the double range, it is refused or not reliable. Both integrands take ηx·e^(βu) as one exponential, so neither leaves the double range before their peak. A time at or below 0, where QuantLib's density divides by 0, is refused; at x at or below 0 the distribution is QuantLib's 0, and below 0 the density is 0. The stationary forms are 0 below 0 too, where QuantLib's powers of a negative size give NaN, or at λ = β a negative probability.

The stationary distribution, JumpSizeDistribution(double), is QuantLib's: a series below a cut and the stationary density's integral above it. QuantLib's series at min(x, ε) holds the mass to min(x, ε)/η, so with η below 1 and x below ε/η its integral is empty and it returns the mass to ε/η or x/η, not x (4.9% to 151% off); there the series is taken at ηx (a fix, a documented divergence), QuantLib's bits elsewhere.
