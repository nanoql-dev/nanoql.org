Remarks

The method takes the underlying method's constraint at zero, weights, optimization method and L2 penalties, and its own cut-off times; the underlying method's own cut-offs apply to its part. When the discounting curve has another reference date than the fitted curve, its discount is rebased to the fitted curve's reference date. A discounting curve that changes is a new curve: QuantLib's method follows its handle.

QuantLib reads the discounting curve at the fitted curve's time (nonlinearfittingmethods.cpp:414-416), which the discounting curve counts from its own reference date on its own day counter: dated 30 days earlier, the fitted discount misses the rebased discounting curve by up to 3.1e-3, and by up to 1.05e-2 on Act/360. Here the discounting curve is read at the instant the fitted curve's time denotes; where the two curves share their reference date and day counter it is QuantLib's read and bits. A documented divergence.
